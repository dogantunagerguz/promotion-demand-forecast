# 2. Explanations you ruled out

| Explanation considered | How tested | Why ruled out |
|---|---|---|
| June is seasonally high for categories 1, 9 and 11; the baseline was not low, the forecast period was high. | Weekly sales of the same weeks in 2024. | 2024 is flat from February to July; in 2025 the drop starts with the stock drop and ends when stock returns. |
| The April drop was a demand drop, not a supply problem. | Weekly opening stock, sales and empty-shelf rate, all stores. | Opening stock fell by about 70 % in categories 1, 9 and 11 (12 % chain-wide) while sales did not rise and shelves emptied far more often (empty-shelf rate 12 % → 49 % in those categories); a demand drop would not empty shelves. |
| Excluding empty-shelf days biases the lift because those are the high-demand days. | Lift computed three ways on the same 647 events: all days, empty days excluded, empty days filled from the same promotion's stocked store-days. | The two corrections differ by about 4 %; empty-shelf days have low opening stock and follow the delivery cycle (lowest Thursday, highest Sunday) rather than demand. |
| Six items launched on 3 March pulled the baseline down with low opening weeks. | Weekly sales from 3 March; their P18 errors. | Ramp-up ended on 24 March, the window starts on 31 March; the six items' P18 errors are between −6 % and +4 %. |

| Where the interval comes from | The same method was applied to twelve past promotions with only the data available at their own origin. Demand came out above the forecast in eleven of the twelve (P07 1 % below; on average 7 %, the demand hidden by empty shelves), so the promotion-week forecast is multiplied by 1.074; the central 80 % of the demand/forecast ratios (1.05–1.11) is applied to the half of the P19 volume that has a precedent. - Looked before computing: in Power BI I saw the post-promotion sales dip, the April 2025 sales drop with its rise in stock-constrained days, and which categories carried the P18 error (11, 9, 1 under, 3 over); each was later confirmed by a table in the notebook.
